# Polite chaser

## When to use

Any time something is overdue and needs a nudge: an unpaid invoice, an email that's gone unanswered, a document waiting for a signature, or a delivery that's late. Also useful for a supplier who owes the person something.

## What you need

- The specifics: invoice number, amount, the date it was originally due or sent, who it's to.
- context/people.md, to set the tone. A long-standing client who's always paid on time gets a gentler nudge than a new contact who's already late twice.

## The 4-step ladder

Chasing works best as a ladder that gets firmer in stages, not the same tone repeated. Match the step to how much time has passed and how many nudges have already gone out.

### Step 1: Friendly nudge

When: a few days to a week after something was due, first time asking.
Tone: light, assumes it's just been missed, no pressure.

Invoice example: "Hi Tom, quick one, invoice 1042 for the March catering (£340) was due last Friday. Might have slipped through, so flagging it in case. Let me know if you need anything from me to get it paid."

Slow reply example: "Hi Priya, following up on the quote I sent over on the 2nd, no rush if you're still deciding, just didn't want it to get buried."

### Step 2: Clear reminder with specifics

When: one to two weeks after step 1, no response.
Tone: still warm, but states the facts plainly and asks for a response.

Invoice example: "Hi Tom, invoice 1042 (£340, originally due 20 March) is still outstanding. Could you let me know when it'll be settled, or if there's a problem with it I should know about?"

Slow reply example: "Hi Priya, checking in again on the quote from the 2nd. I need a yes or no by Friday to hold the slot. Let me know where things stand."

### Step 3: Firm, with a date

When: two to four weeks after step 1, or after step 2 goes unanswered.
Tone: direct, states a specific date it needs resolving by, no small talk.

Invoice example: "Hi Tom, invoice 1042 (£340, due 20 March, now 3 weeks overdue) still hasn't come through. I need this settled by Friday the 24th. If there's an issue, let me know today so we can sort it."

Slow reply example: "Hi Priya, I haven't heard back on the quote from the 2nd despite 2 follow-ups. I need an answer by Wednesday or I'll have to release the slot to another job."

### Step 4: Final, before escalating

When: well past the original due date, previous steps ignored.
Tone: calm, formal, states what happens next if nothing changes.

Invoice example: "Hi Tom, invoice 1042 (£340, due 20 March) is now 6 weeks overdue, and I've sent 2 reminders with no response. If it's not settled by Friday the 31st, I'll need to look at next steps, including late payment charges. Let me know if there's a reason it hasn't been paid."

Slow reply example: "Hi Priya, I've chased this 3 times now with no response, so I'm going to assume the quote from the 2nd is no longer wanted and release the slot from Friday. Let me know if that's wrong."

## Rules

- Always include the specifics: invoice number, amount, original date, and how many times it's been chased. Vague chasers get ignored; specific ones get paid.
- Match the tone to the relationship in context/people.md. A client who's always reliable and just slow this once gets step 1 even if it's been a while; someone who's ignored two reminders already starts at step 3.
- Run the human-email checklist on every draft, whatever the step.
- Draft only. The person sends it. Never chase anyone automatically or without being asked.
- For UK business-to-business invoices, you may mention that statutory interest and fixed compensation can apply to late payment of commercial debts under the Late Payment of Commercial Debts (Interest) Act 1998. Mention it only at step 4 at the earliest, don't quote a specific rate or amount, and tell the person to check what applies to their own situation before using it.

## If something is missing

If you don't have the invoice number, amount or original date, ask for them before drafting; a chaser without specifics isn't worth sending. If context/people.md doesn't cover this contact, ask the person how firm to be, or default to starting at step 1.
