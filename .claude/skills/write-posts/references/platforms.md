# What each platform allows and rewards

One place for the rules of each platform. Every content skill points here rather than repeating them. The limits below are the app's own (`CM.PLATFORMS` in `apps/content/app/model.js`); the rest is general guidance for a small business. Platforms change their rules, so treat the limits as a safe working guide and, if the person says a platform now allows something different, believe them and tell them to update the number in `docs/CUSTOMISING.md`.

What each platform "rewards" is what's commonly seen to work, not a promise. Never quote a statistic, a percentage or a "best time to post" as fact. If it matters, say it comes from the person's own research, and name the source.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Instagram | `instagram` | 2,200 characters | photo, carousel, reel, story | instagram.com |

## Instagram

- **Shape**: the first line has to work on its own, because the rest is hidden behind "more". Then a few short lines. A clear next step at the end ("message us", "link in bio").
- **Formats**: a photo of real work; a carousel (several slides, each one idea, first slide is the hook, last slide says what to do); a reel (a short vertical video, 15 to 30 seconds is a safe default); a story (casual, disappears after 24 hours, good for behind the scenes).
- **Rewards**: real photos and video of real work, people and places, over stock pictures. Carousels people swipe through. Posts people save or send to someone.
- **Rhythm**: 2 to 3 posts a week is plenty for a small business. Stories are optional extras.
- **Watch out**: the caption limit includes hashtags. Use 3 to 5 relevant hashtags at most, at the end, or none. Never a wall of them.

## TikTok

- **Shape**: the caption is short (a line or two). The work is in the video: what happens in the first 2 seconds, and a spoken or on-screen hook.
- **Formats**: video only. A short how-to, a before and after, a day in the life, answering one question customers ask.
- **Rewards**: a clear hook, one idea per video, real people and real work, videos people watch to the end. TikTok is also used as a search engine, so a video that answers a specific question can keep finding people.
- **Rhythm**: 2 to 3 videos a week if they can keep it up. Otherwise 1 a week is better than a burst and a gap.
- **Watch out**: it needs a video, so it always adds a `video` need unless the person already has one. Never suggest a trending sound or dance unless it fits the business. Using a sound or music in a business account can be restricted; tell them to use the platform's own business-safe options.

## LinkedIn

- **Shape**: the first 2 or 3 lines show before "see more", so the first line carries the point. Short paragraphs, plain words, written by a person to a person. One idea per post.
- **Formats**: text; a photo; a carousel (a PDF or slides someone scrolls through); a video.
- **Rewards**: a real story, a lesson from real work, a straight opinion, a customer's own words. Posts that start a sensible conversation.
- **Rhythm**: 2 to 3 posts a week is a sound rhythm.
- **Watch out**: no "thrilled to announce". No "agree?" bait at the end. A post that reads like a press release gets skipped. If there is a link, many people put it in the first comment instead of the post itself. Offer that, don't insist.

## Facebook

- **Shape**: friendly and local. Short is fine; the very long limit is not a target.
- **Formats**: text; a photo (an album for a job); a video; a reel.
- **Rewards**: photos of real work, local news, customer stories, offers and events people in the area care about. Posts people comment on and share.
- **Rhythm**: 2 to 3 posts a week.
- **Watch out**: a page's posts often reach only a small share of its followers, so local groups the business already belongs to and shares in (never spam them) are often where the replies are. Check each group's own rules before posting.

## YouTube Shorts

- **Shape**: the title is the thing that counts: 100 characters at most, plain, with the words someone would search for. The description is optional.
- **Formats**: a short vertical video. Under 60 seconds is the safe default.
- **Rewards**: a hook in the first couple of seconds, one clear answer, titles that match what people type into search.
- **Rhythm**: 1 to 2 a week, often the same video as TikTok or an Instagram reel with a different title. Check you have the right to use any music.
- **Watch out**: in the app the first line of the text is the title, and the count is against 100 characters. Keep the title to its own first line.

## Google Business Profile

- **Shape**: plain, local and useful, 1,500 characters at most, but much shorter is better. Say what, where and when. Add a button (such as Call or Learn more) when the post asks for an action.
- **Formats**: an update (news, a finished job, a seasonal reminder); an offer (needs a real offer with real dates); an event (needs a real date).
- **Rewards**: fresh, relevant posts with a real photo show people searching locally that the business is active. Searches are often local and ready to buy, so be clear and specific about the service and the area.
- **Rhythm**: 1 a week is enough.
- **Watch out**: never invent an offer or an event. Keep phone numbers out of the text and use the profile's own Call and Learn more buttons instead. Posts can be reviewed and refused if they break Google's rules, so keep to plain facts.

## X

- **Shape**: 280 characters at most, including any link. One thought, said plainly.
- **Formats**: text; a photo.
- **Rewards**: being quick, useful and specific; replies and conversation. A thread is several connected posts, which the app handles as separate posts, so don't write threads unless asked.
- **Rhythm**: only worth it if their customers are really on X. 3 posts a week is plenty to try.
- **Watch out**: count the characters. Don't pad. Don't post the same text as LinkedIn, because the tone is different.

## Email newsletter

- **Shape**: a short subject line (aim for under 50 characters, and say what's inside), then one idea in plain words, one clear thing to do, signed by the person. Short.
- **Formats**: email.
- **Rewards**: being useful to people who asked to hear from you: one helpful tip, a recent job, an honest note. Sounding like one person writing to another.
- **Rhythm**: every 1 to 2 weeks, and only as often as they can keep up.
- **Watch out**: in the UK they may only send marketing emails to people who agreed to receive them (or, in some cases, to existing customers who were offered a way to say no), and every email needs a working way to unsubscribe. Say so once if they ask about adding people to a list, and tell them to check the ICO's own guidance (ico.org.uk). Never say this is legal advice. The email goes out from the person's own email tool; Claude never sends it.

## Fitting a platform: checks before you save a post

1. The text is within the limit. Count it. For YouTube the first line is the title and it's within 100.
2. The format is one of the formats for that platform.
3. The first line stands on its own (Instagram, LinkedIn, Facebook, the subject line of an email, the title for YouTube).
4. Anything the post needs from the person (a photo, a video, a quote, a case study) is in `needs`, not assumed.
5. Nothing is a real claim you can't back up: a number, a result or a customer's words come from the person or from a source you can name.
6. It passes the voice checklist in `.claude/skills/write-posts/SKILL.md`.

## What a score of 0, 1 or 2 means, per factor

Used by `plan-my-content` and `write-posts`. Use only what is in front of you (the research, the post and what the person told you).

| Factor | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| `fit` | The format is a poor match for the platform (a long essay as a TikTok) | It works but isn't what the platform is built for | The format is a natural fit (a 30 second how-to on TikTok) |
| `demand` | Nothing in research shows people want this | Related interest in research, or a loose match | A search or topic in research matches directly |
| `hook` | The first line or first 2 seconds are generic | A fair opening | A specific, curious or useful opening that would stop a scroll |
| `proof` | Nothing real in it, and nothing like what works for competitors | One of: matches what works for competitors on this platform, or has some real proof | Real proof: a photo of real work, a real number or a customer's words, and it matches what works |
| `timing` | No reason for now | A general season or occasion | In season, or tied to a real date or something being talked about now |
