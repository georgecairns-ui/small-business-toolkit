# Delegate it

## When to use

When a task on the person's plate would be better done by someone else: a team member, a freelancer, a supplier, an assistant. The person wants a clear brief and a message to send, not just a one-line instruction that leaves the other person guessing.

## What you need

- The task itself, including anything already known about it (deadline, who it's for, materials already produced).
- context/people.md, to know who could take this on, how to address them, and how formal to be.
- context/business.md, for anything about budget or how the business usually works with freelancers or suppliers.
- The task list entry, if one already exists, so you can update it rather than create a duplicate.

## Steps

1. **Check who the task is for.** If the person hasn't said, check context/people.md for a plausible match (a team member with the right skills, a freelancer who's done similar work before) and suggest it, but don't assume without checking.
2. **Build the brief** with these parts:
   - **Outcome**: what needs to exist or happen when this is done, in plain terms.
   - **Why it matters**: enough context that the person doing it can make good judgement calls without checking back on every small thing.
   - **What done looks like**: a concrete description, not "make it good", so there's no argument later about whether it's finished.
   - **Deadline**: a real date, not "soon" or "whenever you can".
   - **Materials and where they are**: files, links, passwords they'd need pointed to, logins they're allowed to access.
   - **Who to ask**: a named person if they get stuck.
   - **Budget or time box**: if there's a cap on hours or spend, say it plainly so nobody oversteps it by accident.
3. **Draft the handover message** in the person's voice using write-like-me and human-email, built from the brief above but written as a message, not a bulleted spec sheet, unless the receiving person is used to bullets (check context/people.md).
4. **Offer to update the task list**: set the task's category to delegate and fill in who it's going to, and offer to add a check-in later under waiting, once the brief is sent, so nothing gets forgotten. Propose this; don't change the task list until the person says yes.
5. **Present the brief and the message together**, so the person can see both before anything goes anywhere.

## Rules

- Never send the handover message. Draft only.
- Never change a task's category, owner or check-in without the person's explicit yes first; offer it, don't assume it.
- Be concrete about budget and time box if one exists. Leaving it vague is how small jobs become expensive ones.
- If the task genuinely isn't ready to hand off (missing a decision only the person can make, missing materials that don't exist yet), say so rather than drafting a brief with gaps in it.

## Examples

Delegating to a team member: "Brief for Sam: get the March invoices out to all Fern & Finch clients. Why it matters: cash flow's been tight this month, so getting these out on time matters more than usual. Done looks like: every client from the list invoiced, correct amounts, due date of 14 days from send. Deadline: Thursday. Materials: the client list and rates are in the finance folder. Ask: come to you if a rate looks wrong. No budget needed, this is inside Sam's normal hours. Want me to set this task to delegate, assigned to Sam, and draft the handover message?"

Delegating to a freelancer: "Brief for the freelance designer: redo the Hollins Lettings flyer with the new logo. Why it matters: the old flyer still has the previous branding and it's going out to print next month. Done looks like: a print-ready PDF at A5, new logo, same text as the old flyer unless they flag something that reads oddly. Deadline: 2 weeks from today. Materials: old flyer and new logo are in the Hollins Lettings client folder. Budget: cap it at £150. Ask: come back to you with questions, not guess."

## If something is missing

If there's no obvious person to delegate to, say so and ask who, rather than guessing at a name. If there's no deadline yet, ask for one; a brief without a date is just a task restated.
